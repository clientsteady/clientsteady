01
What Happened
The 4 developments that matter this period
Critical Conversion rate collapsed across all paid channels mid-month CVR: 2.8% → 1.4%
What the data shows
Conversion rate dropped from 2.8% to 1.4% between June 30 and July 9 — a 50% decline that held flat for the rest of the month. The drop was simultaneous across Google Search, Performance Max, and Meta, which rules out platform-specific causes. Click volume was stable. The problem is between the click and the purchase.
What changed on July 9
A website update was pushed on July 8 (confirmed in Shopify changelog). Checkout flow was modified — the shipping estimate step was moved from cart to post-email-entry. Session recordings show a sharp increase in cart abandonments at the shipping reveal step, consistent with sticker shock on delivery costs. This is the most likely primary cause.
Critical Performance Max cannibalized branded search — budget wasted on lowest-funnel traffic Brand CPC +84%
What the data shows
Branded keyword CPC increased from $0.62 to $1.14 while branded impression share dropped 22 points. PMax campaign is competing for the same branded queries with no exclusions in place. Estimated wasted budget: $3,200–4,100 on traffic the client would have received organically.
Why this matters now
With ROAS already compressed by the CVR issue, paying a premium for branded clicks that convert at lower rates (because PMax sends them to category pages, not product pages) is compounding the problem. The client is spending more to acquire customers it would have acquired for free — and those customers are still hitting a broken checkout.
Warning Top 3 Meta creatives reached frequency threshold — CTR declining Freq: 4.8 · CTR –31%
What the data shows
The three ads driving 71% of Meta spend have average frequency of 4.8 in the last 21 days. CTR on these creatives dropped from 1.9% to 1.3%. CPM has risen 18% as Meta's algorithm signals audience exhaustion. New creative assets were not introduced in July.
Urgency
This issue is secondary to the CVR problem — fixing creative before fixing checkout will not recover ROAS. However, creative fatigue will become the primary issue within a few weeks if new assets are not launched. Flag this for the next 30 days once the CVR situation is resolved.
Monitor Google Shopping impression share recovered — no action needed IS: 38% → 52%
Context
Shopping impression share recovered from a brief dip in late June. Likely caused by a temporary competitor budget increase that has since normalized. No structural issue — this should hold through August at current bid levels. Worth noting positively to the client as evidence of competitive positioning improvement.

🔍
Analytical Confidence
The checkout change explanation accounts for the timing, the cross-channel nature of the drop, and the session behavior pattern. This is the leading hypothesis and the best-supported explanation given the available data, but it is not yet confirmed — a controlled revert or A/B test would confirm it. The PMax brand issue is confirmed but additive, not causal to the CVR decline. We cannot rule out a seasonal component without prior-year data — worth requesting from the client.
03
What This Means Commercially
Separating signal from noise — what is actually at risk
⚠️
Revenue at Risk (This Month)
The CVR decline from 2.8% to 1.4% on $41,200 spend is associated with approximately $45,400 in lower revenue relative to June performance (a simple comparison, not a causal estimate). If the decline persists, August would be expected to show a similar shortfall. This is not a media efficiency problem — it is a website problem presenting as a campaign problem.
💡
What Can Be Fixed Immediately
Two issues are entirely fixable this week without additional spend: (1) revert or optimize the checkout flow to restore shipping transparency earlier in the flow, and (2) add branded keyword exclusions to PMax. If the leading hypothesis holds, these two changes address the main identified issues; we would size the recovery only after seeing post-fix data.
📊
What is Noise
The CPM increases, the Shopping IS fluctuation, and the minor CPC changes are not the story this month. These are normal market movements. Do not let the client focus the conversation on bid strategy or audience targeting — the issue is upstream of campaign optimization.

The Client Conversation
What Ironclad will ask — and how to answer
They'll ask Why did ROAS drop so significantly? Is the agency underperforming?
How to answer: "The ROAS decline is not a media performance issue — it's a conversion rate issue. Overall clicks and impressions were stable this month (Meta creative fatigue is a separate, secondary issue we are tracking). The drop happened because conversion rate fell by 50% starting July 9, which is the day after a change was made to the checkout flow on your site. We identified the likely cause — shipping costs are now revealed later in the checkout process, and session data shows customers abandoning at that point. We want to work with your team to test reverting that change this week, and if the hypothesis holds we would expect conversion rate to improve; we would want to see post-change data before putting a number or a timeline on the recovery."
They'll ask Why did you keep spending if performance was declining?
How to answer: "The decline materialized over the first two weeks of the month, and our monitoring flagged it as an emerging issue in the second week of the month. (Fill in the actual alert date from your monitoring log before using this answer with a real client.) We began investigating the cause immediately — identifying that it was a site-side issue rather than a campaign issue was important because the fix is different. Pausing spend wouldn't have recovered revenue; fixing the checkout will. We've now isolated the root cause and have specific actions ready to implement this week."
They'll ask What's your forecast for August if we fix this?
How to answer: "If checkout is restored to pre-July 8 behavior and branded exclusions are added to Performance Max, we would expect CVR to move back toward its pre-change range, but we would not put a number or date on it yet. We'd want to see at least 7 days of post-fix data before committing to any August forecast. We'd also recommend reviewing prior-year July data together — if July has seasonal softness in your category, we should calibrate expectations accordingly."
They'll ask Should we reduce spend while this is happening?
How to answer: "It depends on cash flow timing. The checkout fix is the higher-leverage move — fixing the funnel is likely to matter more than any spend adjustment right now. If cash flow is a concern, a modest temporary reduction (15–20%) while the fix is validated is reasonable. However, reducing spend significantly during this period risks losing auction position that will be expensive to rebuild. Our recommendation is to prioritize the checkout fix and maintain spend while that's being tested."
06
Decision Status
Where this stands and what happens next
Decision Status
Elevated Attention Required
A 34% single-month revenue decline with a well-supported leading hypothesis and an actionable test. This isn't a scoring output — it's a judgment call based on the evidence in Sections 1–3: the checkout-flow change is documented (Shopify changelog + session recordings), the timing lines up closely, and the test is within the client's control. Its causal role is the leading hypothesis, not a confirmed result. Recommend raising this with the client proactively, before end of week.
✓
Recommended Action
Lead the conversation proactively with a clear root-cause explanation, then propose testing the checkout revert together this week. Framing this as a joint fix — not just a report — keeps the client oriented toward solving the problem rather than assigning blame.
⏸
What Not To Do Yet
Don't commit to a specific August revenue recovery number before seeing post-fix data, and don't reduce media spend meaningfully — that would compound the problem without addressing its cause.
🔍
What Would Make This Diagnosis Wrong
If prior-year July data shows a comparable seasonal dip independent of any site change, the checkout-flow explanation would need to be revised downward in severity (see Section 2 — this factor is currently unconfirmed, not ruled out). If the checkout revert test doesn't recover CVR within 10–14 days, that would point to a second, undiagnosed factor.
